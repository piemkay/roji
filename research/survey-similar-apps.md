# Survey: calm, ADHD-friendly task apps like Quest

Research for [#44](https://github.com/piemkay/roji/issues/44) on the map [#43](https://github.com/piemkay/roji/issues/43).
Gathered 2026-11-24 from vendor sites, docs, app store listings and independent reviews. Vendor
pages are marketing: claims about ADHD fit and user counts are the vendor's own. Where a page did
not cover a point, the entry says so rather than guessing.

## Question

Which apps are closest to the calm, low-friction, ADHD-friendly to-do app Roji wants to be? For
each: core idea, capture, day planning, how the backlog is kept from growing, automation, and what
users praise and complain about. Then: which are worth a deep dive that are not already ticketed.

## Short answer

No app does all of what Roji wants (capture from anywhere including email, automatic sorting, a
short suggested Today, no review rituals, no ever-growing backlog). The field splits into four
families:

1. **Calendar-first auto-schedulers** (Motion, Akiflow, Reclaim, Morgen): strong capture from
   email/Slack and automatic placement on a calendar; expensive, busy, built for knowledge workers
   rather than for calm.
2. **Ritual planners** (Sunsama, Quest): a calm, intentional daily plan, but the calm comes from a
   manual planning ritual, the very thing Roji wants to drop.
3. **Visual / timeboxed day planners for ADHD** (Tiimo, Structured, Llama Life): excellent at
   "what now, and for how long", weak at capture and at the backlog.
4. **Lists that manage themselves** (Lunatask, Twos, plus a long tail of "expiring task" apps):
   the closest to Roji's "no ever-growing backlog" idea. Lunatask hides tasks that are not
   relevant now and lets them rise with age; Twos rolls unfinished items over automatically;
   indie apps delete or archive tasks after a fixed time.

Around these sit **helpers** (Goblin Tools: break a task down, turn a brain dump into actions,
estimate time) and **general task managers often recommended for ADHD** (Things, TickTick,
Todoist, Amazing Marvin). Recurring praise across the field: fast capture, one thing at a time,
visible time, feeling of progress. Recurring complaints: price and subscriptions, setup and
options overload, cluttered interfaces, rigid auto-scheduling, and bugs in timers/sync.

## Already ticketed (brief)

**Quest** ([quest.app](https://quest.app)). Long-term goals ("quests") connect to a daily plan;
tracked time adds up as "quest hours". Capture is a keyboard-first "New task" shortcut (N). Day
planning is a weekly planner with the advice to put the most important task in "before the small
things fill the day", plus a focus mode (F) with one task and a session timer. No stated backlog
mechanism beyond reviewing weekly time per goal. No AI mentioned; local-first, offline once
prepared. Free during beta; web plus macOS and Linux desktop apps. Too new for meaningful
independent reviews.

**Sunsama** ([sunsama.com](https://www.sunsama.com)). "The digital daily planner that helps you
feel calm and stay focused." Pulls work into one list from calendars, task tools (Asana, Linear,
Todoist, Jira...), starred Gmail and Slack/Teams messages. A guided morning planning ritual and an
evening shutdown ("End work on time, without guilt"). The assistant Sunny finds waiting work and
adjusts the day; Zapier and a remote MCP server. Zapier's reviewer praises the guided flow that
reviews overdue and backlogged tasks but notes it is harder for people with time blindness, since
it relies on good time estimates; $20/month ([Zapier](https://zapier.com/blog/adhd-to-do-list/)).

**Tiimo** ([tiimoapp.com](https://www.tiimoapp.com)). "A visual AI planner", "built by and for
neurodivergent people". Brain dump by typing or voice; an AI co-planner turns it into steps with
time estimates; the day is a visual timeline with visual timers. No backlog mechanism described.
Free core, Pro for AI breakdown. iOS, Android, web. App Store about 4.6 from ~20k reviews; Apple
iPhone App of the Year 2025. Complaints: listed as free but pushes the subscription, sync and
timer bugs, tasks reported disappearing ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/tiimo/id1480220328),
[TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/websites-apps/timo)).

**Llama Life** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/adhd-organizer-llama-life/id6454469750)).
A timeboxed list: every task gets a countdown timer, one task at a time, and the app shows the
time of day you will be done with the whole list ("End time"), which ADHD reviewers single out.
"Break it down" and a "Not Now" list exist. About $6/month or $39/year. Complaints: alarm and
chime bugs after switching between web and app.

**Motion** ([usemotion.com](https://www.usemotion.com)). AI auto-scheduler that "dynamically
optimizes your schedule dozens of times a day". Captures from forwarded email, meetings,
Slack/Teams, Siri. Now a broad suite (projects, docs, meeting notetaker). Praised for removing
decision fatigue; criticised for price (reported $29-$69/seat/month), cluttered dashboards and
rigid rescheduling ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/214264/Motion/),
[G2](https://www.g2.com/products/motionapp/reviews)). It is the opposite of calm, and it writes to
the calendar, which Roji has ruled out.

## Further apps

**Akiflow** ([akiflow.com](https://www.akiflow.com)). Tasks and calendars in one app with an AI
assistant. Capture is its strength: a Universal Inbox fed by Gmail (one click), Slack, two-way
Notion/Jira sync, Zapier, Apple Watch; Claude can connect through the Akiflow MCP to pull action
items from meetings. Day planning is time-blocking into free time, with "Smart Time Slots"
holding several tasks and drag between Today / This week / Next week. Backlog: views for this
week, this month and "someday". Automation: AI assigns projects to incoming tasks, auto-schedules,
scheduled workflows. No free plan: $34/month or $19/month yearly
([pricing](https://akiflow.com/pricing)). Reviewers praise it once learned but call it expensive,
not intuitive at first, "danger of over organising", weak mobile app, one-way Slack/ClickUp sync
([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/232035/Akiflow/reviews/)).

**Things 3** ([culturedcode.com/things](https://culturedcode.com/things/)). The calm-design
benchmark: two Apple Design Awards, 2026 App Store Awards finalist. Everything lands in an Inbox
(global Quick Entry shortcut on Mac, Siri); lists are Today, Upcoming, Anytime and Someday;
calendar events can show inside Today ([guide](https://culturedcode.com/things/guide/)). The
backlog is held off only by the Someday list, which the user is told to review periodically: a
manual ritual. Automation is via Apple Shortcuts. One-off purchase per platform, Apple only.

**Structured** ([structured.app](https://structured.app),
[App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/structured-day-planner/id1499198946)). Tasks and calendar
on one visual timeline. Capture into an Inbox to sort later, or import the calendar. Pro adds "plan
with AI" and "Replan", which reschedules missed tasks automatically. Pomodoro timer, widgets.
Subscription or $99.99 lifetime. 4.8 from ~169k ratings; reviewers with ADHD say "nothing has ever
worked for me the way this app has" and praise the Inbox; requests are for travel time and for
unexpected tasks to push later ones.

**Lunatask** ([lunatask.app](https://lunatask.app)). Encrypted to-do list, habits, journal and
notes, "designed specifically with ADHD brains in mind". Capture by forwarding email, a browser
extension and a quick note. Day planning drags tasks into free calendar time; time-blocking and
timers. Its backlog idea is the closest to Roji's: tasks that are not relevant now stay out of
view and "as time goes by, they gain relevance and rise to your attention"; automatic
prioritization by age, status and priority, a work-in-progress limit, and a "reset age" action
([docs](https://lunatask.app/docs/features/tasks/workflows/review-old-tasks),
[release 1.6.5](https://lunatask.app/releases/1.6.5)). Zapier and a public API. Free tier;
Premium $6-8/month; $300 lifetime. Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android. End-to-end encrypted,
no analytics (vendor claim).

**Twos** ([twosapp.com](https://www.twosapp.com)). Write freely; Twos suggests whether a line is a
to-do or a note. Capture from email, text, WhatsApp, Slack, browser, Apple Watch. Everything
appears on Today and **unfinished items roll over** to the next day automatically, which Zapier's
ADHD reviewer found "really helpful" for procrastination
([Zapier](https://zapier.com/blog/adhd-to-do-list/)). AI (ask your notes) and coin rewards are in
Twos Plus. Free capture; all major platforms including Linux.

**Amazing Marvin** ([amazingmarvin.com](https://amazingmarvin.com)). "Works with your brain, not
against it": pick "strategies" (300+ settings) to build your own system. Day planning with a
capacity estimator that warns when the day is over-full; Super Focus Mode, Task Jar (random next
task), Spotlight sessions, Procrastination Wizard. Backlog tools are anti-procrastination prompts
rather than automatic pruning. Zapier's reviewer calls it "the best to-do app for ADHD that I have
ever used"; others find the setup overwhelming, which the team answers with premade workflows
([AppSumo reviews](https://appsumo.com/products/amazing-marvin/reviews/)). $12/month.

**TickTick** ([ticktick.com](https://ticktick.com)). All-rounder: natural-language dates, AI voice
capture into several tasks, calendar views, Eisenhower matrix, Pomodoro, habits; an AI assistant
breaks down projects. No backlog mechanism. Praised by Zapier as "remarkably robust" for focus;
calendar and timeboxing need Premium ($35.99/year).

**Todoist Ramble** ([todoist.com/ramble](https://www.todoist.com/ramble)). Not a separate app but a
notable capture pattern: speak freely and an LLM turns speech into tasks with dates, priorities,
projects and labels; 40 languages; input not used for training (vendor claim). Shows how good
"brain dump to tasks" capture can be.

**Goblin Tools** ([goblin.tools](https://goblin.tools)). Single-purpose AI helpers "for when things
feel too big or complicated": Magic ToDo breaks a task into steps (a "spiciness" level sets the
depth), Compiler turns a brain dump into actions, Estimator guesses durations, Taskmaster keeps you
on one task. Free web, cheap apps. Neurodivergent users are enthusiastic; complaints are breakdowns
that are excessive at high spiciness and odd estimates
([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6449003064)).

**Saner.AI** ([saner.ai](https://www.saner.ai)). AI assistant over notes, Gmail, Slack, Drive and
calendar, "built by people with ADHD"; turns brain dumps into plans and surfaces emails to follow
up. Web and Chrome extension only. Young; claims are vendor claims.

**Reclaim** ([reclaim.ai](https://reclaim.ai)) and **Morgen** ([morgen.so](https://www.morgen.so)).
Calendar-first schedulers that place tasks from other tools into free time. Notable details:
Reclaim lets you preview and approve AI changes before they apply; Morgen flags tasks at risk of
being late, reminds you of unfinished ones and runs on Linux. Both write to the calendar.

**Superlist** ([superlist.com](https://www.superlist.com)). Clean tasks-plus-notes app with voice
"Talk" capture and an MCP endpoint. Nothing specific on backlog or ADHD.

**Finch** (via [Zapier](https://zapier.com/blog/adhd-to-do-list/)). Self-care pet app with goals;
notable only for gamification that keeps ADHD users coming back. Mobile only.

**Expiring-task apps** (Done & Dusted, Slate, "gone", must-bye, Do or Die). Small indie apps that
wipe the list at midnight or delete/archive tasks after 1-7 days. They prove the "no backlog" idea
has an audience but none is a mature product
([example](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6763887989),
[Do or Die](https://github.com/lkang-pdx/do-or-die)).

## Patterns worth carrying into Roji

- **Capture** that works best: one global inbox fed by email and chat (Akiflow, Sunsama, Motion,
  Lunatask), and free-form text or voice parsed by a model (Todoist Ramble, Twos, Tiimo, Goblin
  Compiler).
- **Today**: the calm apps keep it small and visual and show when you will be done (Llama Life end
  time, Structured timeline, Marvin capacity warning).
- **Backlog**: only Lunatask (relevance rises with age, hidden until then) and Twos (auto rollover)
  automate it in a mature product; the rest rely on Someday lists and reviews. Auto-delete exists
  only in indie apps.
- **AI that users accept** previews before acting (Reclaim, Morgen) and breaks things down on
  request (Goblin, Tiimo). Fully automatic rescheduling (Motion) is the most criticised.
- **What drives people away**: price, setup and options overload, busy interfaces, bugs in timers
  and sync.

## Recommended deep dives (not yet ticketed)

- **Lunatask**: the nearest mature take on a self-managing backlog (now/later, age-based priority,
  WIP limit), plus email capture, E2EE and Linux.
- **Akiflow**: the reference for capture from email and chat into one inbox, and an MCP
  connector, both in Roji's MVP scope.
- **Twos**: frictionless capture that classifies note vs to-do, and automatic rollover.
- **Structured**: inbox-to-timeline flow and "Replan" with a very large ADHD user base; what makes
  it stick.
- **Goblin Tools**: the AI helpers (breakdown, brain-dump compiler, estimator) that are candidates
  for Roji's model features.
- **Amazing Marvin**: its catalogue of anti-procrastination strategies, and the warning of what
  too much configurability does.
- **Things 3**: the calm-design benchmark for list structure and Today.
